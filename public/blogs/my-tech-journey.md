# From Projects to Purpose: My Journey in Tech

My journey with computers started way before I knew what “software engineering” really means.

Back in 7th grade, I had a strong interest in computers and laptops. I loved exploring systems, settings, and just figuring out how things work. At that time, I didn’t know much about coding or programming. I just knew one thing: I enjoy being around computers.

After school, I wanted to go for FSc, but due to low marks, that path didn’t work out. Instead, I took admission in BS Software Engineering. Looking back, that decision changed everything for me.

## Learning Beyond the Classroom

College gave me the foundation, but most of my **practical skills came from outside**.  
YouTube tutorials, online courses, bootcamps, and official docs were my real teachers.

I was very curious. I kept asking myself:

-   What if I build this?
-   What if I try that?
-   How does this system actually work?

My first real freelance project was for a **South African client**, where I worked on a **blockchain-based school donation system using crypto**. At that time, my confidence was high. I felt like I could learn and build almost anything.

But slowly, reality hit.

## The Phase of Chasing Everything

As I moved forward, I realized one hard truth:  
**Software engineering is a very vast field.**

Web apps, mobile apps, dashboards, backend, DevOps, testing, automation, AI — everything looked important. I wanted to learn all of it. And honestly, that mindset was exciting but also very exhausting.

Over time, my confidence went down a bit. Not because I was bad — but because I realized nobody can master everything. Still, my hope never went to zero.

Even today, sometimes I feel lost. Sometimes I feel burnt out. And that’s normal.

## Entering the Real World

The real shift happened when I started my **job** and worked on **production-level systems**.

That’s where I truly tested my skills.

The corporate and startup world taught me things that personal projects never could:

-   Deadlines are real
-   Ownership matters
-   Responsibility is heavy
-   Impact is more important than perfection

I learned that it’s not just about completing tasks or shipping features. It’s about **solving real problems**, making decisions, and owning the outcome.

This is where my thinking changed.

## From Learning Everything to Learning What Matters

At one point, I had to accept something honestly:  
Trying to learn everything was not feasible. It was a mad decision.

So I changed my approach.

Now, I focus on learning **what actually matters**, and then polishing those skills on the go. Instead of chasing every new shiny thing, I focus on depth, fundamentals, and real-world problem solving.

This mindset also shaped how I see AI.

## AI, Skills, and Reality

When AI started booming, it made me think deeply about the future of programming and my own skills.

One thing became very clear to me:  
AI is powerful, but **AI alone is not the solution**.

What really matters is:

-   Understanding systems from scratch
-   Knowing why something works, not just how
-   Designing solutions, not just generating code

AI is a tool. Skills are the foundation.  
And that foundation cannot be replaced easily.

## Where I Am Now

Today, I work more with **AI agents, chatbots, and intelligent systems**, but my approach is very different from before.

I don’t just build projects to finish them.
I build to:

-   Solve real problems
-   Create leverage with AI
-   Deliver impact for businesses and founders

I still struggle with burnout. I still feel tired sometimes, especially when I don’t have enough time to work on side projects outside my job. But now I’m more honest with myself.

I’m no longer chasing random projects.

## What I’m Really Chasing

Today, I’m chasing:

-   The **success of meaningful projects**
-   And most importantly, **my peace of mind**

This journey from projects to purpose shaped me not just as a developer, but as a person. I’m still learning, still growing — but now with direction.

And that makes all the difference.
